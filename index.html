Lingo Captions: privacy policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

Lingo Captions turns speech into captions, transcripts and translations on your own device. The app has no account, no ads and no analytics of its own, and the developer receives none of your audio, text or pictures.

What stays on your device

  • Your recordings, captions, transcripts, translations, saved words, dictionaries and settings are kept in the app's own storage on your device.
  • You can delete a conversation, with its transcript and recording, from your device in History at any time. Removing the app removes everything it kept on your device.
  • The microphone is used only while you run captions, dictation or a recording, and the app shows when it is listening.
  • Your own microphone recordings are kept so you can play them back; Keep recordings in Settings turns this off.
  • In the App Store, Google Play and Microsoft Store versions, sound captured from other apps (System captions, and Group or Calls captions, which mix it with the microphone) is kept only so the app can improve its transcript and, on Windows, identify its speakers, and it is never played back. On iPhone, iPad and Android it is deleted as soon as an Improve has used it, and on Windows once Improve and Identify Speakers have both used it; otherwise 24 hours after the recording ends, or the first time the app runs after that. The Mac App Store version improves the transcript by itself when captions stop, then deletes the sound.
  • Android 12 and newer only: when you move to a new phone with a device-to-device transfer that you start, and the phone's transfer tool supports it, everything the app keeps goes with you except the speech models and the models that read text in pictures, which can be downloaded again. On Android 10 and 11 the app's data is not transferred. The app's data is never included in Android's cloud backup.
  • On iPhone and iPad, everything the app keeps is included in your own iCloud or computer backup, like other apps' data, except your recordings and the speech models; the models can be downloaded again after a restore.

What leaves your device, and when

  • Downloads you start or agree to: speech models, voices, dictionaries and the models that read text in pictures come from public servers (Hugging Face, GitHub and each dictionary's own site). As with any download, those servers see your device's internet address. The app sends nothing about you or your content.
  • Once a day the app asks Hugging Face whether the speech models you have downloaded have changed, so it can offer updates. As with any download, the server sees your device's internet address and which model files are checked. You can turn this check off in the app's settings.
  • Android only: translation and reading text in pictures and PDFs use Google's ML Kit, which works on your device. Your audio, text and pictures stay on the device, but while these features run, ML Kit sends Google diagnostic information, such as performance figures, error codes, the device and app version, the languages chosen for translation, and an identifier for this installation of the app. It is sent encrypted, and Google says it does not pass it on to others. ML Kit's translation models are downloaded from Google. Google describes this here: ML Kit data disclosure.
  • Where your device has them, the app uses Apple's or Google's own speech recognition, translation and text features in their on-device modes. They are part of the system and follow Apple's and Google's privacy policies.
  • Links you paste: the app downloads the media from the site you named, which sees the request as it would from a browser.
  • Send Log (Report a problem), in the Windows, Mac, iPhone and iPad apps: only when you choose it. It opens your own email app with the recent run logs attached, and you decide whether to send them. The logs say which version and device ran and what the app did, with any errors; they hold no recordings or transcripts, but may name files you opened. The Android app has no Send Log: Google Play shows the developer crash reports (the app version, the device, and where in the app the crash happened) from devices whose owners share usage and diagnostics data with Google.
  • Purchases: the free trial and the purchase go through Google Play, the Microsoft Store or the App Store. The app only learns whether the full version is bought; your payment details stay with the store.

Children

  • The app collects no personal information from anyone, children included.

Recording other people

  • Tell people before you record or caption a conversation with them. The rules differ from country to country.

Changes

  • If this policy changes, the new version is posted on this page with its date.
