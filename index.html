Lingo Captions: privacy policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

Lingo Captions turns speech into captions, transcripts and translations right on your own device. There is no account and there are no ads, and the app doesn't track how you use it.

Lingo Captions doesn't collect your data

  • The developer never receives your audio, your text or your pictures. Everything you make in the app (recordings, captions, transcripts, translations, saved words, dictionaries and settings) is kept in the app's own storage on your device.
  • Copies only go to places that are yours: a file you export, or your own iCloud or Google backup. You'll find the details for your device below.
  • You can delete any conversation, with its transcript and recording, in History whenever you like. If you remove the app, everything it kept goes with it.
  • The app only listens while you use captions, dictation or a recording, and it always shows you when it's listening.
  • Your own microphone recordings are kept so you can play them back. If you'd rather not keep them, turn off Keep recordings in Settings.
  • When the app captions sound from other apps, that sound is never played back. In the App Store, Google Play and Microsoft Store versions it's only kept for a short time, to improve the transcript. You'll find the details for your device below.
  • Export everything (in History) puts the text of your conversations and your saved words into one file, saved wherever you choose. Import brings it back. The file has no recordings in it, and it only leaves your device if you save or send it somewhere else.

When the app uses the internet

  • Downloads: speech models, voices, dictionaries and the models that read text in pictures come from public sites (Hugging Face, GitHub and each dictionary's own site), only when you start or agree to a download. Like any download, those sites see your device's internet address. The app sends nothing about you or what you've made.
  • Model updates: once a day the app asks Hugging Face whether your downloaded speech models have a new version. Hugging Face sees your internet address and which model files were checked. You can turn this off in Settings.
  • Links you paste: the app downloads the media from that site, just as a browser would.
  • Your device's own features: where your device has them, the app uses Apple's or Google's own speech recognition, translation and text features, in their on-device mode. They're part of your system and follow Apple's or Google's privacy policy.
  • Send Log (Report a problem), in the Windows, Mac, iPhone and iPad apps: nothing is sent unless you choose it. It opens your own email app with recent logs attached, and you decide whether to send them. The logs show the app version, the device, what the app did and any errors. They don't include recordings or transcripts, but they may include the names of files you opened.
  • Purchases: the free trial and the purchase go through the App Store, Google Play or the Microsoft Store. The app only learns whether you've bought the full version; your payment details stay with the store.

iPhone, iPad and Mac

  • Sound from other apps (System captions; on the Mac also Group captions in Calls): on iPhone and iPad it's deleted as soon as Improve has used it, and no later than 24 hours after the recording ends (or the next time you open the app after that). The Mac App Store version improves the transcript by itself when captions stop, then deletes the sound.
  • iCloud backup of History, only if you turn it on (iPhone, iPad and the Mac App Store version): the text of your conversations and your saved words are copied to the app's folder in your own iCloud Drive, so your other iPhone, iPad or Mac with the same Apple ID gets them too. Recordings, models and settings are never copied. Apple keeps these files under its own privacy policy, and the developer never receives them. If you turn the backup off in Settings, copying stops; files already in iCloud Drive stay there until you delete them.
  • Your iPhone or iPad backup: your own iCloud or computer backup includes the app's data, like other apps', except your recordings and the speech models. You can download the models again after a restore.

Android

  • Sound from other apps (System captions): it's deleted as soon as Improve has used it, and no later than 24 hours after the recording ends (or the next time you open the app after that).
  • Translation, and reading text in pictures and PDFs, use Google's ML Kit, which runs on your device. Your audio, text and pictures stay on your device. While these features run, ML Kit sends Google some technical information: performance figures, error codes, your device and app version, the languages you translate between, and an ID for this copy of the app. It's sent encrypted, and Google says it doesn't share it with anyone. ML Kit's translation models are downloaded from Google. Google explains it here: ML Kit data disclosure.
  • Moving to a new phone: if you move with a phone-to-phone transfer that you start, and the phone's transfer tool supports it, everything the app keeps comes with you, except the speech models and the models that read text in pictures, which you can download again.
  • Backup to your Google account: your History and saved words are included only when that backup is end-to-end encrypted, which needs a screen lock on your phone. Recordings, models, dictionaries and settings are never included. Google keeps the backup under its own privacy policy, and the developer never receives it. You can turn Google backup off in Android's settings.
  • There's no Send Log on Android. Instead, if you share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Google Play shows the developer crash reports: the app version, the device, and where in the app the crash happened.

Windows

  • Sound from other apps (System captions, and Group captions in Calls, which mix it with your microphone), in the Microsoft Store version: it's kept to improve the transcript and to tell the speakers apart (Identify Speakers). It's deleted once both have used it, and no later than 24 hours after the recording ends (or the next time you open the app after that).

Children

  • The app doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included.
  • Where a law requires it (for example in California and Brazil from 2027), the app asks Google Play or the App Store for your age range when it starts. Lingo Captions doesn't use, keep or send the answer.

Recording other people

  • Please let people know before you record or caption a conversation with them. The rules are different from country to country.

Changes

  • If this policy changes, the developer will post the new version on this page, with its date.
